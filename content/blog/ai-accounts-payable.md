Open the shared payables inbox at most small businesses on a Thursday and you'll find the same mix. A supplier invoice sent twice, once as a PDF and once as a link. A freelancer's bill with no PO number. A statement from the office supply company showing two invoices nobody remembers getting. And somewhere in there, a polite email from a supplier saying they've moved banks and asking you to update their details before the next payment.

Accounts payable looks like data entry from the outside. Most of the work is checking: is this bill real, is it a duplicate, did we get what it says, who has to approve it, and is the account we're about to pay the one that belongs to this supplier. An AI Employee is good at that kind of checking, as long as it has clear rules about where to stop. This post covers what an AI accounts payable clerk does, where it hands off, how one is set up and what it costs next to a hire.

## What an AI accounts payable clerk is

It's an [AI Employee](/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee) that holds one seat: the money the business owes. It reports to whoever owns payables today, usually the controller, the bookkeeper or the owner. It has a login to the accounting software and the payables inbox, read-only access to the bank feed, and a written set of rules about what it can do alone and what needs a person. Everything it does goes into a log.

It's the mirror image of the job in my [AI accounts receivable](/blog/ai-accounts-receivable) post. Receivables chases money coming in. Payables controls money going out, which is why the rules here are tighter. A late reminder is embarrassing. A payment to the wrong account is usually gone.

## Accounts payable, duty by duty

I'd split the job into six duties and give each one an authority level.

![Each accounts payable duty mapped to what the AI Employee does and its authority level](/blog/ai-accounts-payable/03-duties.jpg)

### Getting every bill in

Bills arrive everywhere. A marketing agency gets freelancer invoices by email and media bills through ad platform dashboards. A property manager gets maintenance invoices, utility bills for vacant units, the landscaping contract and the elevator inspection. An online store gets supplier invoices, freight bills and a monthly statement from its fulfilment warehouse.

The AI clerk collects all of them into one queue, reads each into the accounting software (QuickBooks Online, Xero, NetSuite or whatever you use), and checks it against what's already there. The same invoice number from the same supplier, or the same amount from the same supplier within a few days, gets held as a likely duplicate. Duplicates are one of the most common ways small businesses overpay, and they're tedious for a person to catch.

### Coding each bill

Every bill needs an account, often a class or location, and the right tax treatment. A clinic group splits its supply orders by location. A law firm separates client disbursements, like filing fees or an expert's invoice, from its own overhead so they can be billed back. The AI clerk codes from the supplier's history and your chart of accounts, and anything new or unusual goes on a list for the bookkeeper with its best guess attached.

### Matching to the order and the receipt

If you use purchase orders, this is where most of the value is. The AI clerk compares the bill to the PO and to what was received. A distributor's bill for 40 cases when the receiving record shows 36 gets held, and the AI clerk emails the supplier to ask for a corrected invoice or a credit note. Businesses without POs can still match against a contract or last month's amount, so a software renewal that jumped 30% doesn't slip through.

### Getting it approved

Approval is where bills go to sit. The AI clerk sends each bill to the person whose approval it needs, based on rules you set, and follows up when it's been waiting too long. It doesn't approve anything above the amount you allow, and it never approves a bill from a supplier it hasn't paid before.

![An example approval routing table for bills by amount and type](/blog/ai-accounts-payable/04-routing.jpg)

### Answering suppliers

"When will we be paid?" is the most common email in any payables inbox. The AI clerk answers from the records: approved on the 12th, scheduled for the 20th payment run, or waiting on a corrected invoice. It also reconciles supplier statements each month, which is how you find the invoice that never arrived before it turns into a late fee.

### Preparing the payment run

On the day you pay suppliers, the AI clerk builds the batch: approved bills that are due, plus any where paying early earns a discount worth taking. It checks each line against the approved bill and the supplier's bank details on file, and sends the batch to a person. That person reviews it and releases it in the bank with their own login.

## What stays with a person

I'd keep the list short and firm.

- Releasing money. The AI clerk prepares the payment. A person sends it.
- Any change to a supplier's bank details. These get confirmed by phone, to a number already on file, by someone on your team.
- New suppliers. A person checks that the business exists and signs off before the first payment.
- Disputes with a supplier over price, quality or terms.
- Bills over your approval limits, and anything that doesn't match its order.

The bank-change rule is the one I'd never bend. The [FBI's 2024 Internet Crime Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2024_IC3Report.pdf) counted $2.77 billion in reported losses from business email compromise, where criminals pose as a supplier or an executive and ask for payment to a new account. The [Association for Financial Professionals](https://www.financialprofessionals.org/about/learn-more/press-releases/Details/over-75-percent-of-us-firms-experienced-payments-fraud-in-2025-while-ai-adoption-for-fraud-mitigation-lags) found that 76% of US organizations faced attempted or actual payments fraud in 2025.

![Payments fraud numbers from the FBI and the Association for Financial Professionals](/blog/ai-accounts-payable/05-fraud.jpg)

An AI Employee helps here in one specific way: it reads every bill, so it notices when an invoice arrives with bank details that differ from the supplier's record, or from a sender address one letter off from the usual one. It flags those and holds the bill. What it can't do is confirm the change is real. That takes a phone call from a person.

The overnight bills are read, coded and matched before the operations manager is in. The freight bill that came in twice is held. The supplier's bank change, the short shipment and the payment batch all reach a person, each with what they need to decide.

## What a payables hire costs

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics counts accounts payable clerks within [bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/bookkeeping-accounting-and-auditing-clerks.htm). The median wage for that group was $50,670 a year, or $24.36 an hour, in May 2025.

Wages are about 70% of what private employers spend on compensation, according to the BLS [Employer Costs for Employee Compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm) report. At the clerk median, that puts the full cost near $72,400 a year before recruiting and training.

![What an accounts payable seat costs a business each year](/blog/ai-accounts-payable/07-cost.jpg)

Many small businesses have no payables clerk. The bookkeeper enters bills when they get to them and the owner approves everything from their phone at night. The cost there shows up as late fees and the occasional bill paid twice.

## How much you'd pay for an AI accounts payable clerk

There are two ways to get one.

**Building it yourself.** Bill-pay tools and AP automation software read invoices and run approval workflows for a monthly subscription, and AI agent platforms can handle the emails around them. Someone still has to connect the systems, write the coding and approval rules, test it on real bills and keep it current when suppliers and limits change.

**Having it built and maintained.** A provider connects your systems, learns your suppliers and your chart of accounts, and keeps it updated, usually for a monthly fee.

The price depends on how many bills arrive in a month, how many come on paper or through supplier portals, whether you use purchase orders, and how many companies or locations it pays for. A consultancy with 60 bills a month needs much less than a distributor with 1,500.

When you compare quotes, ask who confirms bank changes and who updates the rules when your approval limits change. Then set the number against the full cost of the seat, about $72,400 at the US median, or against the hours your bookkeeper and you spend on bills now.

## How an AI accounts payable clerk is set up

Most of the setup is writing down rules your business already follows without having written them.

![How an AI accounts payable clerk is set up, in six steps](/blog/ai-accounts-payable/08-setup.jpg)

The supplier list takes longest. Every business has a few suppliers who bill oddly: the one who sends a statement but no invoices, the one whose amounts change every month, the one who needs a W-9 before they'll ship. I'd write those down first. Then I'd run it in shadow mode for two or three weeks, where it codes and routes every bill as a draft and a person checks each one, until the drafts stop needing corrections.

## When a person should own payables

If you pay a dozen suppliers a month and know each of them by name, an AI accounts payable clerk is more than you need. Your bookkeeper can handle it in an afternoon. Some websites offer [free assessments](/assessment) that check whether a role can be filled by an AI Employee, which helps when you're unsure where your volume sits.

If bills arrive daily from dozens of suppliers, approvals sit for a week and nobody has time to reconcile supplier statements, the seat is mostly reading, checking and following up. An AI Employee can hold it, with a person releasing every payment.

Before you decide, pull last quarter's bills and count three things: how many arrived without the information needed to approve them, how many waited more than a week for approval, and how many you paid late. That count is the work an AI accounts payable clerk would take on. And whatever you decide, write down today who on your team confirms a supplier's bank change, and how. That rule protects you with or without an AI Employee.
