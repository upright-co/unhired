If you run a business in Alberta and you haven't used AI for anything yet, you are in the majority by a wide margin.

## The number

ATB Economics reported the figure on June 2, 2026, using a Statistics Canada survey. 18.4% of Alberta businesses said they had used AI in the previous 12 months. Across Canada it was 19.2%.

So Alberta sits a little under the national rate, and more than 8 in 10 businesses here have not started.

The survey covers businesses of every size and doesn't break out small ones separately. In a small business the owner is usually the one who would have to find the time to try it, which is a big part of why many haven't.

## Why most haven't

The reasons owners give are usually the same few. They don't know where it would fit. They worry about putting customer information into it. They tried a chatbot once, got a bland answer, and moved on. Or they are simply too busy running the business to test anything new.

Those are fair reasons, and none of them means AI can't help. They mostly mean nobody has shown them a first step small enough to try on a Tuesday afternoon.

## Three places to start that cost almost nothing

**The email you dread writing.** A late-payment reminder, a reply to an unhappy customer, a quote follow-up. Paste in the situation, ask for a draft, then edit it in your own voice. It takes about five minutes.

**The document nobody reads.** A lease, a supplier contract, a long government form. Ask an AI assistant to summarize it and list the dates and dollar amounts. Then check those against the document yourself.

**The job you do the same way every week.** Scheduling posts, turning notes into an invoice, answering the same five customer questions. Write down the steps once, and ask the AI to do the first draft each week.

## Two rules while you try it

Don't paste in anything you wouldn't email to a stranger, such as health information, banking details or a customer's personal records, unless you have checked how the tool handles data.

And check its work. AI assistants are fast and usually right, and they are sometimes confidently wrong. Treat the output like a draft from a new hire.

## What I take from the number

A year or two from now that 18.4% will be much higher. Today it means the business down the street probably hasn't started either. An owner who spends a few hours this month learning what AI is good and bad at will be ahead of most of their market, and it costs very little to find out.
