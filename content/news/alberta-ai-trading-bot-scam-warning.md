Ads for AI trading apps that promise steady returns with no risk are all over social media. On November 3, the Alberta Securities Commission put out an investor alert about exactly that.

## What the ASC says is happening

The ASC is the province's regulator for investments. Its alert says fraudsters are using AI, algorithms and trading bots as a marketing hook to make investment scams look believable. They exaggerate or invent AI's role in their results, sell access to a so-called AI trading platform, or offer to trade for you. Some impersonate a real company or a real investment professional.

Victims are found through social media, online ads, messaging apps such as WhatsApp and Telegram, and personal relationships.

## How it plays out

The pitch says a private AI system can predict markets, trade faster than people, remove human error and earn high returns with little or no risk. There is a polished website, a dashboard, screenshots and testimonials.

The ASC says those dashboards can be created or manipulated to show deposits, trades and profits that never happened. You are asked to send money or crypto, buy access, or give them control of a trading account.

Then comes the part that hooks people. A small early withdrawal goes through, which builds trust. When you try to take out more, it gets delayed or blocked until you pay a fee or a "tax." The ASC says those payments are part of the scam and the money doesn't come back.

## The warning signs

- The person, firm or platform is not registered.
- Phrases like "AI-powered" or "algorithmic trading" paired with "no risk," "high success rates" or "24/7 automated trading."
- Secret or vague explanations of how the technology works.
- Promised or unusually steady returns.
- Bronze, silver and gold packages.
- Countdown timers, limited spots, or pop-ups saying someone just invested.

## What to do

Look up anyone selling you an investment on the National Registration Search, and check CheckFirst.ca and the ASC's Investment Caution List. Don't trust a registration number or certificate they send you, because those can be faked too. Don't install software they say is required, and never share banking, crypto or account passwords.

A friend who swears it worked is not proof. Often they are early in the same scam and haven't hit the blocked withdrawal yet.

You don't need to be sure it is a scam to report it. The ASC takes reports online at asc.ca and at 1-877-355-4488.

## My take

I build AI systems for a living. AI is useful for a lot of work, and none of it involves a secret bot that beats the stock market with no risk. If such a thing existed, the people who built it would not be selling it to strangers on Telegram.
