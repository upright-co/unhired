Most people in Alberta have heard of ChatGPT. Fewer have heard of Claude, and almost nobody outside the tech world could name the company that makes it. This week that company had one of the largest stock market launches ever.

## What happened

The company is Anthropic. On November 18 it priced its initial public offering at $83 a share, above the $70 to $78 range it had expected, according to CNBC. It sold about 980 million shares and raised $81.3 billion. That values the whole company at roughly $1.5 trillion.

Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD are investing a further $26 billion at the same price. The shares began trading on the Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker ANT.

## What an IPO is, in one paragraph

An IPO is the first time a company sells its shares to the public. Before it, only private investors could own a piece. After it, anyone with a brokerage account can. The company gets the money raised, and from then on it has to publish its results every three months.

## What Anthropic sells

Claude is an AI assistant. You type or talk to it, and it writes, summarizes, answers questions, reads documents and works through tasks on a computer. Businesses pay for it by the month or by how much they use it.

So the $1.5 trillion figure is a bet about office work. Investors are saying they expect software like this to do a large share of the writing, sorting, answering and scheduling that people do at desks today.

## What it means if you run a business here

I would take three things from it.

First, this is not a fad. A bet that size can turn out to be wrong, but companies and investors don't put $81 billion behind something they expect to fade in a year or two. If you have been waiting to see whether AI sticks around, I'd stop waiting.

Second, the tools will keep improving, and you won't have to go looking for them. They are being built into email, accounting software, phone systems and booking tools you already pay for.

Third, everyone gets the same tools. A two-person shop in Red Deer can buy the same AI as a national chain. The advantage goes to whoever learns to use it well, and that's a matter of practice more than budget.

## What it does not mean

It is not a reason to buy the stock, and nothing here is investment advice. A high valuation tells you what investors hope for. It doesn't tell you whether AI is ready for a particular job in your business. You find that out by trying it on one small, real task.

A good first test is an hour of your own week. Pick one thing you do every week that is mostly typing, and see how far an AI assistant gets with it.
