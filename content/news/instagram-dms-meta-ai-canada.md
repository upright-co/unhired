If you use Instagram, a new contact is about to show up in your messages, and it is software.

## What launched

On November 24, Meta announced that Instagram users can chat with its AI assistant, Muse, in a direct message thread, TechCrunch reported. It began rolling out that day on iPhone and Android in five countries: the United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore.

Until now you had to leave Instagram and open the separate Muse app to use it. Conversations you have with Muse inside Instagram will also show up in that app as a side chat.

## What it does

According to Meta, you can:

- Ask it for more context about a post, a place or a product you come across.
- Ask it to catch you up on conversations you may have missed in your other DMs.
- Share a post with it and ask it to act, such as adding a coffee shop to a trip plan or finding a Black Friday deal on a pair of shoes.
- Have it sort your saved posts and reels into folders called Collections, turn a recipe video into a grocery list, or build a trip itinerary from travel videos you saved.

When you save a post, Instagram will now show a prompt offering to have Muse organize it. That part is launching in the United States and Canada.

## What Meta promises

Meta says users stay in control. Nothing is shared, published or sent without your approval, the company says, and you can ask Muse to forget information or undo an action at any time.

## The part I'd think about

The feature that stands out to me is the catch-up. To summarize the conversations you missed, the assistant has to read them. Those threads include other people: your sister, your kid's coach, a customer who messaged your shop's account. They didn't agree to anything.

That doesn't make it sinister. Email apps have summarized threads for a while. But a group chat with family is more personal than a work inbox, and I'd want to know what is kept and for how long before I asked an AI to read mine. TechCrunch's report doesn't say, so I'd look for the answer in the app's settings before using that feature.

If you run a business that takes orders or bookings through Instagram messages, there is a second thing to consider. Your customers may soon be asking an AI about your posts and products before they ever message you. Clear prices, hours and locations in your captions and profile are what it will have to go on.

## What to do this week

When Muse appears in your messages, you don't have to use it. If you want to try it, start with the low-stakes jobs, like sorting saved recipes. Leave the catch-up feature until you have read what it stores. And if you share an account with staff or family, agree on whether anyone turns it on.
