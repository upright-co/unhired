Alberta tabled new rules for AI data centres on November 18. The biggest one already approved in the province will not have to follow them.

## The project

Meta, the company that owns Facebook and Instagram, is building a data centre campus in Sturgeon County, just north of Edmonton. It is a $13 billion project planned at 1 gigawatt, and 970 megawatts of grid power have already been approved for it, according to The Canadian Press and the Edmonton Journal.

To give that some scale, a gigawatt is a thousand megawatts. The new rules apply to anything that draws 10 megawatts or more, so this campus is about a hundred times the size where the rules begin.

## The new rules

Bill 42 would keep large data centres at least 600 metres from the nearest home and off irrigated land and prime farmland. It would require close to zero water use for cooling, and it would make companies pay for their own power infrastructure.

The bill has not passed yet, and its details will be set later in regulation.

## Why Meta is exempt

The project was approved before the bill was introduced, so it is grandfathered. That is the normal way new rules work. A government that changes the terms on a project after approving it makes every other investor wonder whether their approval is safe.

It is also why some residents are frustrated. The rules arrived after the largest project was already through the door.

## How many projects are affected

About 70 data centre projects are in the province's concierge program, which helps companies through the process. The government says roughly 30% of them could be affected by Bill 42.

Projects in industrial zones, on brownfield sites or on existing power plant sites are exempt from the 600 metre setback.

## The two sides

People who support the exemption say Alberta asked these companies to come, and that a deal has to stay a deal. Billions of dollars and a lot of construction work depend on companies trusting that.

People who oppose it point out that the concerns behind the bill, which are power, water, noise and farmland, apply to a 1 gigawatt campus more than to anything else on the list.

## What I'd watch

The power rule matters most for anyone who pays a utility bill. The bill says data centre companies cover their own grid costs and that nothing is passed on to ratepayers. How that is written into regulation, and whether it reaches projects already underway, will decide who pays for the lines. I'll keep following it.

Technology Minister Nate Glubish summed up the government's position this way: "We don't need every data centre to get built in Alberta."
