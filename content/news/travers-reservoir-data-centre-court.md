Most data centre fights in Alberta involve a big outside company. This one is between neighbours.

## What happened

In June, Vulcan County council rezoned farmland from rural general to rural industrial so a data centre campus could be built there, the Calgary Herald reported. The applicant is Eric Steeves, who farms in the area. The land is near Travers Reservoir, with the villages of Champion and Lomond nearby.

I'm leaving the size of the project out on purpose. The Herald and CTV News give different figures for the land and the power it would use.

## Why neighbours object

A group of residents formed the Little Bow AI Data Centre Committee and went to the Alberta Court of King's Bench. They want any future data centre proposal to go through what they call "a lawful, transparent and procedurally fair process." CTV News reports they want the rezoning rescinded.

Their case is about the location. They say the area is environmentally significant, that the reservoir matters for irrigation and recreation, and that the site is close to a lakeside community called Little Bow Resort and to the Southern Alberta Bible Camp. They also raised fire risk, the evacuation route, noise, lighting, and air and water pollution.

"It is outrageous that anyone would even consider building a data centre complex or any other industrial development in this unique area," committee member Sylvia Farrant said. "You couldn't pick a more inappropriate spot in the province if you tried."

## What the farmer says

Steeves told the Herald he will keep working with nearby villages and with Little Bow residents. He described the gap this way: "Little Bow residents are there for recreation and having a home that they can enjoy the peace and quiet, so they're going to have a different perspective than a fifth-generation farmer that's trying to bring the tax revenue base and jobs to his local area."

Reeve Jason Schneider said the county will have its day before a judge. "It's an option available to anybody if they feel something wasn't done correctly, and I guess we'll see where it goes."

## Where it stands

On October 6, CTV News reported the group had been awarded a judicial review. That means a judge will look at how the county made its decision. It is a review of the process, and it doesn't settle whether a data centre is a good idea.

Rezoning is also only the first step. Steeves still needs approvals from the Alberta Utilities Commission and the Alberta Electric System Operator, plus a development permit from the county. He told the Herald he doesn't expect the court case to cause major delays, though it adds "another layer of uncertainty and complication."

## What I take from it

Both sides have a point. Rural counties need a tax base, and a landowner trying to build something at home is a different story from a distant company picking a spot on a map. But people chose that lake for the quiet, and a reservoir used for irrigation is a hard place to ask them to trust a promise about water.

The court will look at the process. The location question comes back at the utilities commission and at the county's permit stage.
