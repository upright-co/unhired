If you have been meaning to figure out what AI is and never found the time, the federal government is about to make it hard to keep putting off.

## What was announced

On November 16, federal AI Minister Evan Solomon said a national AI training program will go live in December "for every single Canadian", as reported by BetaKit.

It is free and takes about 90 minutes to complete. It is part of the $13 million National AI Literacy Initiative.

## The Edmonton connection

The initiative is led by Amii, the Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute, which is based in Edmonton. Amii is one of Canada's three national AI institutes and has been part of the University of Alberta's AI research community for more than two decades.

A program meant for every Canadian is being built here, and most Albertans have no idea.

## What we don't know yet

No exact launch date has been given, and the program doesn't have a public name or a sign-up page yet. I'll share the link when it opens.

## Who it is for

From the way it has been described, it is for people who are not technical: a parent wondering what their kids are using for homework, someone worried about their job, a retiree who keeps getting AI-written scam emails, or an owner who hears about AI every day and doesn't know where to begin.

## Why I'd take it, and have a whole team take it

I have built AI systems for businesses for four years. The biggest thing that holds a team back is rarely the technology. Usually half the staff think AI is magic and the other half think it is useless, and both are wrong in ways that cost money.

Ninety minutes of shared basics fixes a lot of that. People learn what these tools are good at, where they make things up, and what should never be typed into one. After that, conversations about using AI at work get more practical, because everyone is starting from the same facts.

If you run a business in Alberta, here is what I'd do. When the course opens, block 90 minutes on the calendar and have everyone take it in the same week, yourself included. Then spend 20 minutes together on one question: what is one task here we should try this on?

The course is free, and the only cost is an hour and a half of everyone's time.
