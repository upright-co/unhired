If you have waited for an MRI in Alberta, you know the scan is quick and the wait to get one is long. The province's new plan goes after the scan time.

On November 12, the Government of Alberta announced nearly $14 million to install AI software on MRI scanners across the province. It expects capacity to rise by 20%, which works out to more than 41,000 additional scans a year.

## How software makes room for more patients

An MRI machine can only see so many people in a day, because each scan takes a set amount of time. This software shortens the scan while keeping the image quality doctors need. Shorter scans mean more appointments on the same machine with the same staff.

The province has not said how many minutes each scan saves, so I won't guess. What it has published is the result.

## What the pilot showed

The pilot began in April 2025 on 10 scanners. Since then, those 10 machines have produced nearly 12,000 scans that would not otherwise have happened.

For context, Alberta did more than 280,000 MRIs in 2025-26, up 4% from the year before.

## Is it safe, and where does my data go?

Two details from the announcement answer the questions most people ask first. The software is approved by Health Canada. And it runs on the scanner itself, so no patient data is sent outside.

It is delivered through Acute Care Alberta's Diagnostic Imaging Alberta program. Health Minister Adriana LaGrange said: "We are committed to helping Albertans get the diagnostic scans they need sooner."

## When it reaches your hospital

The full provincewide rollout is expected in 2028. The province has not published which hospitals come next or in what order.

## Why I think this one matters

Most AI headlines are about chatbots or billion-dollar companies. This story is about a piece of software that was added to equipment Alberta already owns, and that let the same people help thousands more patients.

That is the pattern I'd look for in any workplace, including a small business. Nobody here was replaced, and nothing new was built. The slowest step in the process got faster, and everything after it moved up. If you run a business, it is worth asking where your version of the scan time is.
