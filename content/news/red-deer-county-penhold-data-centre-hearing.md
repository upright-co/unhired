A public hearing in Red Deer County on November 17 drew more than 100 people, filling the council chambers and the lobby outside. They were there to oppose a $300 million AI data centre proposed for the Junction 42 Business Park near Penhold, according to rdnewsNOW and the Red Deer Advocate.

## What is proposed

The company is Wave Infrastructure Ltd., which also goes by Wave Infra. It calls the project an AI research lab. rdnewsNOW reports that the company's website lists a 12 acre site and a data centre that would draw five megawatts of power.

Red Deer County bought the Junction 42 land in 2018 to build a commercial, industrial and agri-business hub.

## What the hearing was about

Council was hearing from the public on two bylaws. One amends the area plan for Junction 42. The other rezones 13.82 acres of undeveloped land from agricultural to medium industrial. Passing them would not approve the data centre. It would make the land eligible for one, and the company would still need other approvals, including any required from the Alberta Utilities Commission.

## The 333 pages

Penhold resident Samantha Lafontaine told council she filed an access to information request on October 2. She asked about water, wastewater, electricity, environmental impacts, emergency services and planning. On November 16, the day before the hearing, she received a 333-page package with what she called substantial redactions.

She asked council to adjourn so residents had time to read it, and dozens of people got up to back her. The county's lawyer said the redactions were made under the province's Access to Information Act and that the county has no authority to overturn them.

## What council decided

Council denied the adjournment. Coun. Lee Girard proposed carrying on with the hearing and extending it to December 1 at 1:30 pm, and council agreed unanimously. "That gives two more weeks to go over the documents that the residents received, and it keeps the public hearing open," he said.

There was a catch. Anyone who spoke on the rezoning on November 17 cannot speak again on December 1, so most people chose to wait. rdnewsNOW reported outbursts from the crowd, and one resident told council, "I think you guys just failed to read the room." Mayor Brent Ramsay said the criticism was fair.

## Where the province's new rules fit

The day after the hearing, Alberta tabled Bill 42. The province says it would set a minimum setback of 600 metres for data centres of 10 megawatts and larger. At five megawatts, this project is half that size, so by the numbers the setback would not apply to it. That leaves the decision with the county.

## What I'd watch

Five megawatts is small next to the gigawatt projects in the news. What filled that room was the process: 333 pages a day ahead, parts blacked out, and a hearing that went on anyway.

I think that is the lesson for any county or company. People will accept a lot when they can see the water, power and noise numbers early. When they can't, they assume the worst. If you live near Penhold or Springbrook and want a say, December 1 at 1:30 pm is the date.
