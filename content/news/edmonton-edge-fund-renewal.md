Edmonton city council is about to weigh a question that splits people at every dinner table: should a city hand out grant money to private companies so they grow here?

## What is on the table

Taproot Edmonton reported on November 24 that council's executive committee will consider renewing the Edmonton Edge Fund. The first version funded early-stage startups and growing companies. The renewed one would change focus to established companies trying to scale up.

It depends on money. The fund only comes back if council approves a package called Edmonton Advantage in its 2027 to 2030 budget, and that package includes $1 million for the Edge Fund. City administration would look for other funding to add to it.

## What the first round paid for

The first phase gave $4.75 million in non-repayable grants to 17 Edmonton companies. Recipients included Future Fields, Zero Point Cryogenics, 48Hour Discovery, Dark Matter Materials, UpRow and Swift Charge.

According to the city's report, as Taproot summarized it, the companies reported:

- $15.4 million in revenue growth, including $5.9 million from exports
- 340 jobs
- $6.79 million spent locally on capital
- 53 products or services brought to market

Nine projects are finished, and the rest are expected to wrap up by the end of 2026. Those numbers are what the companies reported. They don't tell you how many of those jobs would have happened without the grant, which is the hard question with any program like this.

## Who would qualify now

The two old streams become one, for companies that already make revenue or profit. Grants would run from $200,000 to $750,000, cover up to half of a project's cost, and last up to two years.

Eligible fields include advanced manufacturing, AI and technology, tourism, defence, energy and clean tech, food processing and agriculture, health and life sciences, and transportation and logistics.

The first round drew 632 expressions of interest. The city says it wants fewer, bigger grants and fewer applications to sort through.

## The two sides

Supporters say a city competes with every other city for companies that are ready to grow, and matching grants keep that growth here. Critics say businesses should fund their own expansion, and that city money is better spent on roads, transit and policing that every business uses.

## My view

The change to established, revenue-making companies is the right one if the fund comes back. A grant that covers half of a real project, at a company that already has customers, is easier to measure than a bet on an idea. I'd want the city to publish, for each grant, what the company promised and what it delivered.
